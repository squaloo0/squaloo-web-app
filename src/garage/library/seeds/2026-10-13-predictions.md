# Predictions, written before any search
Dated 2026-10-13 09:39 PDT (2026-10-13T16:39:17Z). Not edited after research began.

1. Inference at tiny N — The literature will say that at ~100 real visits/day no test, frequentist or Bayesian, can detect a realistic conversion lift inside a founder's patience window; Bayesian methods will relabel the limit rather than remove it (the prior does the work), and the bot-contamination sources will say bots show up as sample-ratio mismatch and metric inflation that no test corrects after the fact.
2. Agents running shops — Later Project Vend material and Vending-Bench 2 will show scaffolding that raises mean profit while keeping the long-tail collapses (hallucinated deals, forgotten state, over-ordering); critical readings will say the benchmarks measure long-horizon coherence against simulated customers, so nothing in them transfers to real demand.
3. Agent commerce specs — The four specs will share one shape (a buyer agent presents a scoped, merchant-bound payment credential over HTTP), all will be preview/proposed rather than shipped at volume, and none will say anything about demand — only about how an already-willing buyer pays.
4. Micropayments and credits — Szabo/Odlyzko/Shirky will say micropayments fail because the mental cost of deciding exceeds the price; the prepaid-credit literature will say prepayment decouples the pain of paying from consumption, i.e. coins are a mental-accounting device that only works after someone has already decided to buy.
5. Deceptive patterns — Crawl data will put dark patterns at roughly a tenth of shopping sites; regulators will define them by effect on the user's choice, not by UI element, so the boundary for wrasse is a test of effect, not a blacklist of widgets.
6. Discovery for agents — llms.txt will turn out to be unadopted by the major engines; the only measured thing will be crawl-to-referral ratios showing AI crawlers fetch thousands of pages per visitor sent; the real discovery surface will be a structured product feed submitted to one platform.
7. Bots on small sites — First-party write-ups from small infrastructure operators will say AI crawlers are a bandwidth cost, not a demand signal, and scanner studies will say most unsolicited requests to a fresh host are untargeted probes that begin within hours of the host going live.
